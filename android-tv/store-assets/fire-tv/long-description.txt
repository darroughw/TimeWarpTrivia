Nostalgia, weaponized.

TimeWarp Trivia is a party trivia game built around one big screen and a room full of phones. The TV shows the questions, the scoreboard, and the drama — everyone else answers on their own phone, using it like a buzzer. Nobody but the host needs this app; players just open a web page.

HOW IT WORKS

Launch TimeWarp Trivia on your Fire TV and a room code appears on screen. Everyone else grabs their phone, goes to timewarptrivia.com/play in any browser, types in the room code and a nickname, and they're in the lobby — no app store, no download, no account. From 2 up to 10 players can join the same room.

Once everyone's in, the host picks what's in play: filter to a single decade or open up all six at once. Each round puts a question on the big screen with a countdown clock; players lock in an answer from their phone before time runs out. Answering right matters, but answering right fast matters more — TimeWarp Trivia scores on speed, so the fastest correct answer always outscores a slower one.

After the main rounds, the scoreboard turns into a spotlight: whoever's sitting in last place gets pulled aside for a solo question, answered alone while the rest of the room watches. It's the one twist that can flip a game in its final minute — and the reason nobody's safe just because they're not currently losing.

WHAT'S PLAYABLE

Six decades of pop culture, 60s through 2010s, spanning music, movies, TV, toys, and trends — plus Deep Cuts, a growing set of topic packs (currently The West Wing and Fallout) for groups who want something more specific than "everything from the 90s." Thousands of questions across every category means decade combinations rarely repeat the same way twice, so this isn't a game you play once and shelve.

BUILT FOR THE ROOM, NOT THE SCREEN

TimeWarp Trivia deliberately doesn't ask anyone but the host to install anything. That means it works for whoever happens to be in the room — family visiting for a holiday, a game night with mixed phone types, a party where half the guests don't want to download one more app for one more game. Everyone's already carrying the only hardware they need.

Rounds are paced for a real conversation to happen around them: fast enough that the game doesn't drag, slow enough that people actually talk trash between questions. The final scoreboard remembers every round, so bragging rights are earned across the whole game, not just the last question.

No login, no account, no data entry beyond a nickname. Start a game, share a code, and go.
